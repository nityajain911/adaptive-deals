# Adaptive Deals — 90-second demo script

## Setup

Run `npm run reset:seed`, open `/deals` and choose **Start walkthrough**. The first card asks the question the product answers: *A client says your quote doesn't fit their budget. What now?* Select **Repair bookings now, defer social**. Keep the guide open during the recording.

How the guide works: on a merchant step it shows **Your turn** and names the product's own button ("Click “Send 2 questions to buyer”"). **Show me** scrolls to that button and rings it; it never clicks for you. Only simulated buyer and system events have a guide button, and those are violet and labelled. All data, messages and payment events are synthetic.

Each case starts from its recorded Groq run, replayed from stored outputs: no model call is made during the demo. The case cards and the guide show **Recorded Groq output**. If you see **Sample output written by hand · no model ran**, the recordings are not loaded; stop and check `data/case-runs.json`.

## Two more cases to show on request

**Keep the annual scope, move the start (about 40 s).** On **Understand the buyer**, the card reads *We couldn't map “complete annual package” to specific services* and quotes the buyer's words. Offer is blocked until the merchant confirms: tick all four services and select **Confirm required services**. The Offer card then reads *Recalculated by the engine after the merchant confirmed the services · explanation written by code*. Open **Audit details**: Groq extracted the phrase, the merchant confirmed the services, the engine recalculated. Also point to the line saying the GST basis was not captured and that every offer has the same outcome either way. In the chat, “complete annual package” is marked amber: the phrase code could not map.

> “The model returned a phrase that names no service, so code refused to guess. The buyer said they need the whole programme and not to reduce it, so the merchant confirms the services, and only then does the engine recalculate. The model also missed that the buyer had said both amounts exclude GST; here that changes nothing, and the product says so instead of asking.”

**Decline an impossible start date (about 20 s).** Groq's extraction is clean: ₹2,00,000 including GST, a start by the 15th, all four services required. The engine rejects every approved offer.

> “When no approved offer works, the product says so and explains why, instead of inventing one.”

**Answer a lower price with the negotiation corridor (about 60 s; see the next paragraph for the version with discounts).** After the proposal is sent, open the buyer's page, choose **Request a change**, pick **A lower price**, and send "Could you do a lower total: 40000?". On **Track**, open **Buyer work needing a decision**: **Answer with a different total**. Enter 40000, choose **Including GST** and select **Check this amount**. The engine rechecks it and shows the rule it does not meet: *Discount 13.08% is above the 0% limit.* Tick the override, select **Prepare counter-offer**. The Offer card reads **Your counter-offer**, says *You set this amount · the engine rechecked it and found rules not met · you chose to override them*, and lists the overridden rule. Then **Review buyer proposal**, approve and send: the buyer's old link shows *replaced* and version 2 carries the new total.

> “The buyer asks for less. The engine checks my own number and tells me which rule it breaks: my policy allows no discount. It is my call, so I can override, and the product records that as my decision, not the engine's. The buyer then gets a new version to accept, with the same scope and dates.”

**Negotiate within a discount ladder (about 60 s, optional).** This needs the illustrative example, so do it on a fresh deal. Open **Business economics**, select **Load illustrative example: 10% maximum discount with 0%, 5%, 10% approved**, then **Review changes** and **Activate**: the version now reads *Version 2 · Illustrative*. Start a new deal, take it to *sent*, and from the buyer's page ask for a lower price. On **Track** the corridor lists the lowest total that meets every rule, the approved discounts with what you keep and whether the buyer needs MD approval, and other ways to lower the payment (a smaller scope, different timing). Select **Offer this** on the 5% row: the Offer card shows the new version, no override is recorded, and nothing reaches the buyer until you approve and send. Then try a buyer amount below the ladder: choose whether it includes GST, check it, and see which rules fail before any override.

**Prove Commercial Memory on an unseen query (about 35 s, optional).** Open **Presenter → Commercial Memory proof**. Ask “Can monthly reporting be sold by itself?” and select **Test retrieval**. Point out the exact Service delivery rules quote and the **Code retrieval** label. Then select **Use unsupported query** and test again: the product says **Insufficient evidence** instead of filling the gap. Finally select **Show simulated source conflict**: two deliberately conflicting active prices are blocked rather than copied into Business economics. If a Groq key is available, **Try live AI rerank** lets the model reorder only the already eligible chunk IDs; it cannot add evidence or answer the question.

**Show the MCP gate (about 15 s).** After the buyer accepts, point to the **Approved action layer** card on Track. In this environment it says **Razorpay MCP is not connected** and **No Razorpay MCP action occurred**. Then show the separately labelled simulated collection setup. Say: “The product discovered no compatible test tool, so it refused to invent one. With a compatible Razorpay test connection, this same boundary binds the exact agreement and arguments to merchant approval, executes once and stores a reduced receipt.” Do not claim a real MCP call was made.

> “The buyer asks for less. Instead of guessing, the product shows exactly what my own rules allow: the lowest total I can accept, the discounts I pre-approved, or a smaller scope. I choose; the buyer only ever sees the offer I send, never my limits.”

If the illustrative example leaves the demo deal with no feasible offer, skip this paragraph. The walkthrough lists it as an optional step and never activates the economics or sends an offer for you.

## Recording guidance

- Keep the main story on the preventive path. The failed-debit path belongs on `/presenter` for questions.
- In the counter-offer, say that the merchant overrode a rule and that the engine's result still says it failed. Do not say the engine approved it. It applies only before the buyer accepts.
- Say exactly what Groq did and no more. In these recordings Groq extracted constraints and worded the two questions. In the first case its run also ranked the options and wrote per-offer reasons; that order and those reasons are shown only as historical evidence. Code ranks the offers by the merchant's strategy, writes the explanation, and decides every figure, check and approval.
- The recordings are one real run per case, kept as they came, misses included. They are not an evaluation and show nothing about reliability. Do not claim they do.
- Do not say that AI read the payment-change reply. The supported phrase is read by `reply-rules.v1`; unsupported phrases require merchant attention.
- Do not claim a mandate guarantees payment, that the prototype measures actual delivery, or that the frozen evaluation is independent.
- If a step is out of sequence, choose **Restart** in the walkthrough and confirm. The guide returns to case selection and the guided path starts cleanly.
